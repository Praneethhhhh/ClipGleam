# ClipGleam Video Quality Standard

## Approved benchmark

Use the creator-approved [Why We Get Seasons review cut](../output/why-we-get-seasons-review.mp4) as the visual and editorial benchmark. It is an 87.8-second vertical explainer with 16 shots, original graphics, synchronized narration and captions, and sparse original sound accents. See the [editorial storyboard](SEASONS-REFERENCE-STORYBOARD.md) and [approved production review](../output/seasons-production-review.md). The media and generated review files are local artifacts under the ignored output folder.

Meet the same quality bar across topics. Choose the pacing and shot count for the explanation. The benchmark's 16 shots are an example.

## What the viewer should see and hear

- Start with a visual question, contrast, or action in the first five to six seconds. Show the subject immediately. Use original humor when it helps the viewer understand or remember the idea.
- Explain one idea at a time in language a curious ten-year-old can follow. Use familiar examples. Introduce a technical word while the object or action it describes is visible.
- Show recognizable objects and animate what they do. For RAM, show a memory module, chips, contacts and data movement. For a process, show the input, transformation and result. Use labels and icons to identify meaningful details.
- Keep the frame focused. Let the visual carry the explanation. Use short captions for accessibility and brief labels to direct attention. Avoid duplicating the voiceover with paragraphs or leaving a static heading on screen for an entire explanation.
- Make every meaningful movement serve the narration. Cut, zoom, highlight, compare or change the state of an object when the viewer has something new to notice. Maintain spatial continuity when following the same object or process.
- Keep the voice clear and conversational. Use deliberate pauses around difficult ideas. Time music and sound effects to specific events and keep them below speech. Check the hook aloud before committing to the full voiceover.
- Build to the selected duration through the script and shot timings. Use recorded speech duration to adjust cuts. Judge the speed by comprehension as well as energy.
- End with a payoff, recap or callback that answers the opening question.

## Details that teach the right idea

Every color, arrow, marker and change in size should communicate something accurate. Pair color with a label, shape or action so the explanation works for viewers who cannot distinguish the colors.

In the seasons cut, orange and blue dots identify summer and winter. A stronger version could add a small sunlight inset with bright, concentrated rays for summer and a wider, dimmer footprint for winter. Compare the same place in both views, keep the actual Sun the same size in the orbit diagram, and distinguish sunlight angle from daylight duration. The inset communicates the creator's brightness idea without implying that Earth's changing distance from the Sun causes seasons. Treat this as a documented improvement for a future revision of the approved cut.

## Production and review

1. Prepare an idea, shot list, exact planning prompt and visual and sound cues. Get creator approval before sending the planning prompt.
2. Review the generated storyboard for factual accuracy, spoken rhythm, recognizable visuals and a meaningful change in every shot. Resolve missing visual assets or components and get creator approval before narration.
3. Generate the approved script. Use actual speech timestamps for captions and visual events. Preserve the approved text and record any delivery choices. Reuse saved audio when revising visuals.
4. Review an initial export at phone size. Inspect every shot and sample cue transitions. Check clarity with sound on and whether the visuals still make sense muted. Verify labels, captions, contrast, safe margins, motion, continuity and scientific accuracy. Listen for pronunciation, intelligibility and balance.
5. Fix issues found in review and export a separate version. Verify the MP4 decodes end to end and contains the expected dimensions, duration and audio. Record what was verified and what still needs a creator playback check.

A render is ready for creator review once these checks pass. The creator's playback approval establishes the final quality bar for that video.

## Animation and review practices for V1

- Give each action a teaching purpose. Use reusable motion for reveals, paths, comparisons and callouts. Use separate spoken cues within a shot when the viewer needs to see successive changes.
- Use a shared renderer and a small set of trusted visual components. Add specialized components for behavior that requires domain logic, such as computing binary-search states or showing a phase change. Keep the chosen assets and actions explicit in the storyboard.
- Show the actual planned assets in storyboard previews. Inspect representative frames before and after key cues and check layouts with long labels. Bundle a consistent typeface locally. Select the font during visual implementation.
- Validate the storyboard schema, supported actions and cue occurrences. Bind approval to a specific version. Check recorded alignment against the approved script and reuse valid speech when only visuals change.
- Combine automated export checks with human review on a phone. Review accuracy, relevance, motion timing, pronunciation, narration continuity and caption legibility. Use the approved seasons cut as ClipGleam's quality benchmark.
